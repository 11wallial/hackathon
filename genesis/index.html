Overload

Charge is your ammunition, your power, and your death clock. There is only ever so much of it on the field — and the only way to hurt something is to give it yours.

What happened

Rules, in full

A unit is destroyed when its charge goes over its capacity, not when it hits zero. Full is lethal; empty is merely helpless.

Charge is conserved. Nothing is ever created or destroyed. Every point you spend ends up somewhere — in a body, or on the floor.

STEP moves one node and picks up loose charge there, filling you to capacity and leaving the rest.

SHOVE pushes charge into the next node. If something is standing there it absorbs it — and picking charge up can never overload anything, so only a shove or a blast can kill.

Any unit at half capacity or more runs hot and acts twice. That includes you.

An enemy that has spent everything goes to the floor to refill rather than standing around — and it fills to capacity, which leaves it hot and one point from going over. If there is nothing left to pick up, it eventually dissolves: a thing holding nothing is nothing. So there are two ways to end something — give it more than it can hold, or let it spend itself to nothing.

Overloading does not end your run: you detonate where you stand, keep the blast, and lose capacity permanently.
